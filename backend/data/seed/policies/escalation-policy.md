# Escalation & Department Referral Policy

This policy is company-wide and governs when a complaint is referred to another department. The company measures the share of complaints resolved without a referral, so the decision this policy makes is the one the business watches most closely — and the goal is fewer referrals through better knowledge, never fewer referrals through agents guessing.

## 1. When to Refer

A complaint is referred when the remedy needs authority or knowledge the handling agent does not have. Concretely:

- **The remedy exceeds the agent's authority** — authority §2.
- **No policy in this corpus covers the situation**, or two policies point in different directions and the precedence order in kb §2 does not settle it.
- **The facts sit in another department's systems** — a batch history, a fulfilment-centre record, a payment gateway trace, a courier's delivery log.
- **The pattern is bigger than the ticket** — a batch defect, a systemic failure, several customers with one cause (incident §1).
- **A safety or legal trigger is present** — product_safety §1, legal §1. These are referred immediately and are not judgement calls.

A referred ticket is recorded as such at resolution. A ticket resolved from the knowledge base and this corpus alone, with no department consulted, is recorded as handled directly — and that is the outcome the company is trying to make ordinary.

## 2. When Not to Refer

Do not refer because the answer is merely hard to find, because a similar case was referred once before, or because the ticket is unpleasant. Above all, do not refer because the customer is insistent: a customer demanding a manager is asking to be taken seriously, and the correct response is usually a better answer, faster, from the person already holding the ticket. Where the remedy is inside authority and the policy is clear, referral adds days and changes nothing. Two questions settle it: *is the answer already in the corpus?* and *am I allowed to give it?* Two yeses means no referral.

## 3. What a Referral Must Contain

A referral that cannot be answered without a conversation has not saved anyone time. It must state:

- **The question**, specifically — not "please advise" but the decision being asked for.
- **What the customer said**, in their words, and what they have asked for.
- **What has already been checked** — the policy clauses consulted, the diagnostic steps recorded (tech_support §7), the records already pulled (order status, payment trace, tracking history).
- **The remedy under consideration** and why the agent cannot authorise it.
- **What the customer has been told so far**, including any date they have been given.

## 4. Ownership Does Not Transfer

The referring agent keeps the customer. The receiving department answers the *agent*, not the customer, and does not contact them directly — a customer hearing from a department they have never spoken to, about a case they thought someone else owned, experiences it as being passed around. The originating agent decides what of the guidance is appropriate to send, translated out of internal language and stripped of internal detail (comms §3), and keeps the customer updated on the cadence in sla §6.

## 5. What the Receiving Department Owes

An answer within the turnaround in sla §6, addressed to the question asked. The answer must say what the agent is authorised to do, not merely describe the background — guidance that ends without a decision sends the ticket back for a second round. Where the department cannot decide without more information, it asks for exactly that and nothing else. Where the answer changes what agents should do next time, it belongs in a policy revision under kb §5, not only in this ticket.

## 6. One Referral, Then a Lead

A ticket may be referred back for clarification once. If a second round is needed, the ticket goes to a lead, because a question asked twice without a usable answer is a policy gap rather than a communication problem — and the customer has now been waiting through two turnarounds. Repeated referral between two departments on one ticket is prohibited: if ownership is genuinely disputed — an order that never arrived, where shipping, delivery and payments could each claim it is not theirs, is the most common example — a lead assigns it and the assignment is final for that ticket.

## 7. Two Departments at Once, Not in Sequence

Where a complaint needs two departments — a safety report that is also an injury claim, a batch defect that is also a marketplace-channel claim — refer to both in parallel. Serial referral doubles the wait for no benefit. Each department answers its own question; the originating agent reconciles the two, and where they conflict, the precedence order in kb §2 applies. Product safety and legal referrals in particular run alongside the customer remedy rather than gating it: the customer's protection is not held up by the investigation (product_safety §3.2).

## 8. Recording It Honestly

The referral record is whether another department was actually consulted, not how difficult the ticket felt. Marking a self-resolved ticket as referred, or a referred one as direct, corrupts the one metric the company uses to decide where to invest in knowledge — and the second is worse than the first, because it hides a knowledge gap that would otherwise get filled. Where a referral turned out to be unnecessary, record it as referred and note that the answer was already in the corpus: that note is how the corpus gets better (kb §5).
