# Fraud, Abuse & Repeat-Claim Policy

This policy is company-wide and governs the small number of accounts whose claim or order pattern is not explained by bad luck. It is written to protect margin without turning the queue into an interrogation, because the cost of treating honest customers as suspects is far larger than the losses it would prevent.

## 1. Good Faith Is the Default

Every customer is believed. The remedies in this corpus are deliberately evidence-light — a photo instead of a return, a serial lookup instead of a receipt, a stated deadline taken at face value — because the volume of honest claims makes that cheaper than verifying all of them. **No accusation, insinuation or hint of suspicion ever leaves the queue.** An agent who suspects abuse flags the account for review and resolves the ticket in front of them normally; they do not confront the customer, withhold a remedy on suspicion, or write their suspicion in a message the customer may later receive.

## 2. What Triggers a Review

An account, phone number or delivery address is flagged for review, not for refusal, when:

- **Three or more discretionary claims in 12 months** — goodwill gestures, lost-package replacements, courtesy exceptions, or write-off replacements — beyond what one household's ordinary misfortune explains.
- **A repeated COD-refusal or return-to-origin pattern** — orders regularly refused at the door or never collected, especially where the same items are quickly re-ordered.
- **A high rate of "empty box" or wrong-item-inside claims on reverse pickups**, where the doorstep QC in reverse §4 repeatedly finds something other than what should be inside.
- **The same serial number claimed twice** for the same failure, where the first claim was resolved with a replacement or refund.
- **Coupon stacking or wallet cycling** — coupons or referral credit applied in a pattern the terms do not support, or wallet balances moved between accounts to extract value rather than to spend it.
- **A mismatch between the claim and the record** — a delivery scan and photo against a not-received claim, a warranty claim on a serial never sold by us, a claim date before the order date.
- **Clustered claims across accounts** sharing a delivery address, a payment instrument, or a device — see also account §4 for where this overlaps with a compromised account rather than the account holder's own behaviour.

One flag is a note, not a verdict. Patterns are reviewed by a lead; a single agent's impression is not a review.

## 3. What a Review Changes, and What It Cannot

While an account is under review, **discretionary generosity pauses**: goodwill is withheld (retention §4), courtesy exceptions outside the return window are declined, and the next claim of the flagged type requires evidence or lead approval before the remedy is issued. Where COD abuse is confirmed, COD may be withdrawn for that account under orders §6, leaving prepaid payment methods available.

What does not change, at all: **anything the customer is entitled to.** A warranty defect is still remedied under warranty §2, a duplicate charge still refunded under billing §5, a statutory right still honoured under legal §8, and **every safety remedy is issued immediately regardless of any review, flag or pattern** (product_safety §4). We do not put a customer at risk to protect margin. Nor is a review a licence to slow a ticket down: the targets in sla §2 and §3 apply unchanged.

## 4. Courtesy Exceptions Are Logged and Are One-Time

The grace period in returns §3 is a one-time courtesy per account and is recorded as such on the ticket, in the words returns §3 requires. A second request from the same account is a decision rather than a formality: it may still be granted, but knowingly and by a lead. This is the difference between a company that is generous and one that has simply lost track of its own exceptions.

## 5. Chargebacks

Where a customer says they have filed or will file a chargeback, no parallel refund is issued — the process in billing §6 resolves it, and issuing both refunds the customer twice. A pattern of chargebacks filed without contacting us first is a review trigger under §2. Never describe a chargeback to a customer as fraud, an accusation, or a mark against them; it is a mechanism they are entitled to use.

## 6. Units We Did Not Sell

Where a serial number does not appear in our sales records — a grey-market unit, one from an unauthorised reseller, a counterfeit — warranty and returns do not apply, and service is available on a paid basis under spare_parts §4. Tell the customer plainly what we can and cannot do and why, without implying they did anything wrong; most people buying from an unfamiliar marketplace listing do not know who they bought from. **Safety obligations are not conditional on provenance**: a hazard report on a unit we did not sell still receives stop-use instructions, still gets its batch logged, and still reaches Product Safety under product_safety §1.

## 7. Coupon, Referral and Wallet Abuse

Repeated use of single-use coupon codes across accounts, referral rings, and gift-card or wallet testing patterns are handled as configuration and control problems first — the loophole is closed by Sales under sales §3 — and as account matters only where the pattern persists after it is closed. A customer who used a coupon we left open has not committed fraud, and the remedy is to fix the coupon, not to claw back the discount.

## 8. Who Decides, and What the Customer Is Told

Restricting an account, withdrawing COD eligibility as a standing rule, declining future discretionary remedies, or refusing further service is **admin-only**, recorded with the reasoning and the reviewer's name (authority §2). The customer is told the outcome and how to dispute it — that a claim requires evidence, or that we will no longer be able to offer a particular remedy — and never the pattern analysis, the other accounts involved, or what tripped the flag. Where an account restriction is challenged in legal terms, it goes to Legal under legal §1 like any other claim.
