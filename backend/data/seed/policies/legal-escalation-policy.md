# Legal Escalation & Liability Communication Policy

This policy governs complaints that carry legal exposure. It exists because the ordinary instincts of good support — apologise, fix it fast, offer something for the trouble — are the wrong instincts once a claim is live, and the cost of getting it wrong is not measured in the value of the remedy.

## 1. What Must Go to Legal

The following are handed to Legal on identification, before any remedy is discussed:

- **Injury, illness, or property damage** attributed to a product, whether or not the customer uses the word "claim" and whether or not they are asking for anything.
- **A legal notice from an advocate**, or a customer stating they have instructed one.
- **A notice or complaint from the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)**, the Department of Consumer Affairs, or a state consumer helpline.
- **A complaint filed or referenced before a District, State or National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission.**
- **Escalation via the National Consumer Helpline (NCH/INGRAM)** that reaches us as a formal grievance rather than a routine complaint (grievance §3).
- **Contact from a journalist or a customer stating they are going to the press.**
- **A data-protection grievance** under the DPDP Act — access to, correction of, or deletion of personal data, or a complaint about how we have used it (privacy §4).

Recognition is the agent's job; assessment is not. A report that turns out to be minor loses nothing by having been routed here first.

## 2. Language That Does Not Concede

Acknowledge the report, never the cause. Confirm what the customer told us and what we are doing about the report itself, and stop there. Do not write that a product was defective, failed, malfunctioned or was unsafe; do not apologise in terms that attribute the harm to us ("we're so sorry our product burned you"); do not speculate about mechanism; do not compare the report to others. Sympathy for the person is not the same as an admission about the product, and the distinction has to survive being read aloud in a hearing: "I'm sorry to hear you were hurt, and I want to make sure this is looked at properly" is correct. Nothing in this section permits being cold or evasive with someone who has been injured.

## 3. Remedies Freeze While a Claim Is Live

From the moment a report meets §1, the support queue stops offering remedies. No refund, no replacement, no credit, no goodwill gesture, no coupon or wallet credit, no waiver of a fee — not even the ones the agent would normally be authorised to give without asking (authority §4). A remedy issued at this point reads as settlement, and an apology credit reads as an admission. Legal authorises the remedy, in whatever form and at whatever value it determines, and support does not preview it to the customer. Note that this freeze covers discretionary generosity, not statutory entitlements: where the law requires something of us, Legal delivers it — support simply is not the channel.

## 4. Preserve the Evidence

Everything relating to the report is retained from the moment it is identified: the ticket and all correspondence, photos and attachments, the returned unit if we have it, batch and serial records, and the internal investigation notes. Do not ask the customer to return or dispose of the product without Legal's instruction, and do not dispose of a unit already in our possession. **A legal hold overrides the retention schedule in privacy §5 entirely** — nothing on hold is deleted on schedule, and the hold ends only when Legal releases it.

## 5. One Channel of Correspondence

Once a case is with Legal, all correspondence moves to the legal mailbox and support stops replying to the thread — including to messages that look routine, and including to chase-ups. Tell the customer once, plainly, who now owns their case and how to reach them, then hand over the full history so nothing has to be repeated. Two channels answering the same claim is how contradictory statements enter the record. Where the customer keeps writing to support, forward without answering.

## 6. Who May Speak Externally

No one outside Legal makes a statement to a regulator, a consumer commission, a journalist or a customer's advocate, and no one outside Legal and Product Safety uses recall language (product_safety §3.1, §5). This holds for retail, wholesale and marketplace partners speaking about our products too (partner §7). An agent asked a question they are not authorised to answer should say that the question is with our legal team and that they will make sure it reaches them — which is accurate, and better than an improvised answer.

## 7. Consumer Commission Tiers

Once a complaint is filed before a Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, the tier tells Legal how it is likely to proceed and how quickly, and support's role narrows to §5 regardless of the tier. District Commissions handle the majority of consumer complaints and typically move fastest; State and National Commissions hear larger-value and appeal matters and take longer. Support never speculates to a customer about which tier applies, how long it will take, or the likely outcome — that is Legal's assessment to make and communicate.

## 8. The Law Outranks This Corpus

Where the Consumer Protection Act, DPDP Act, or any other applicable law grants a customer more than any policy in this corpus offers — a longer guarantee period, a right of replacement, a refund we would otherwise decline — the law applies and the policy does not. Statutory rights sit at the top of the precedence order in kb §2 for this reason. An agent who believes a customer's statutory rights exceed what our policy allows should escalate here rather than declining, and no policy in this corpus may be cited to a customer as a reason to refuse something the law requires.
