# Wallet, Store Credit & Stored Value Policy

This policy governs money and value we hold on a customer's behalf rather than money already in their bank account: wallet balance, store credit, gift cards, coupons and loyalty points. It answers what each is worth, when it expires, and when it may substitute for a cash refund.

## 1. Wallet and Store Credit

Wallet balance and store credit are usable against any future order and, unlike a refund to a card or bank account, post immediately once issued (billing §4). They may be offered as an alternative to a cash refund or as goodwill (retention §1, §2), but only ever as an alternative the customer actively chooses — see §5.

## 2. Promotional Credit vs. Refunded Credit, and Their Expiries

Credit issued as a **refund** (money the customer is owed back) never expires — it is their money, held in a different form, and an expiry on it would be the same as simply not refunding them after enough time passed. Credit issued as **promotional or goodwill value** (a bonus, a save-offer sweetener, a goodwill gesture the customer accepted as credit rather than cash) expires 12 months from issue, disclosed to the customer at the time it is given. The two must never be merged into one balance without distinguishing which portion is which, because a customer disputing an expired balance is entitled to know whether what expired was ever refundable money in the first place.

## 3. Gift Cards

Gift cards purchased by a customer, or issued by us as a goodwill gesture, are valid for a minimum of **3 years** from issue, consistent with standard consumer-protection expectations for prepaid instruments. A gift card balance is never forfeited for account inactivity, only for reaching its printed expiry, and a customer asking about a balance near expiry is proactively reminded rather than left to lose it silently.

## 4. Coupon Reissue When an Order Is Cancelled or Refunded

Where an order that used a coupon is cancelled or fully refunded, the coupon is reissued to the customer automatically if it was single-use and has not expired, so they are not penalised twice — once by losing the order, again by losing the discount. Any agent may manually trigger a reissue where the automatic process fails, without approval.

## 5. Never Converting a Refund Into Credit Without Consent

A refund the customer is owed is never defaulted to wallet or store credit instead of the original payment method without the customer's explicit choice — offering credit as an option is fine and often genuinely useful to the customer (it posts faster), but substituting it silently, or presenting it as the only option when a cash refund is owed, is not. Where a customer who initially chose credit later asks to convert the remaining balance back to a cash refund, that request is honoured for any portion that originated as a refund under §2; promotional-value credit is not convertible to cash, consistent with how goodwill is issued in the first place (retention §2).

## 6. Loyalty-Point Reversal on Refund and Point Expiry

Where an order that earned loyalty points is later refunded, the points earned on it are reversed, and the customer is told this plainly rather than discovering a balance drop unexplained. Points expire **24 months** from the date they were earned, on a rolling basis, and a customer is notified in advance of points approaching expiry where our systems support it. Points are never reversed as a punitive measure outside of an order-refund reversal — a customer under fraud review per fraud §2 keeps their already-earned points; only future earning or redemption may be paused, and only by admin decision under fraud §8.
