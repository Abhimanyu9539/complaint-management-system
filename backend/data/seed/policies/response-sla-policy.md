# Response & Resolution Time Policy

This policy is company-wide and sets how fast we answer and how fast we finish, by severity. The targets are commitments to the customer, not internal aspirations, which is why the obligation on a target we are going to miss is to say so in advance rather than to explain afterwards.

## 1. The Clock

Business hours are 09:00–18:00, Monday to Saturday, India Standard Time. Public holidays do not consume the clock. The clock starts at the customer's **first** contact about an issue — including a contact later merged as a duplicate (intake §8) — not when the ticket reached the department that will resolve it, and not when someone picked it up. Re-routing, escalation and internal hand-offs do not restart it; from the customer's side, nothing has happened yet.

## 2. First Response

| Severity | First response within |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | 1 hour, 24/7 |
| High | 4 business hours |
| Normal | 1 business day |
| Low | 2 business days |

A first response acknowledges the specific problem, says who owns it, and says what happens next and when. An automated receipt is not a first response. A holding reply is acceptable where the answer needs work — but it must contain a date, and a reply that only asks for information the customer already supplied does not satisfy the target.

These targets are ours, and they are tighter in every case than the statutory acknowledgement floor of 48 hours in grievance §2. The statutory figure is a legal minimum, not a target to aim for — it exists as the backstop that applies if these targets are ever missed, not as the standard we design around.

## 3. Resolution

| Severity | Resolution within |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Same business day, with the immediate risk addressed on first contact |
| High | 2 business days |
| Normal | 5 business days |
| Low | 10 business days |

A ticket is resolved when the customer has the outcome in hand or in flight — the refund initiated, the replacement dispatched, the fix confirmed working — not when we decided what to do. For a critical safety report the customer's immediate protection cannot wait for the resolution target: stop-use instructions and the remedy authorised under product_safety §4 happen on the first contact. All four targets sit comfortably inside the statutory one-month redressal window in grievance §2; that window is what applies if a complaint escalates past us, not the standard we hold ourselves to.

## 4. Missing a Target

The obligation on a target we will not make is to tell the customer **before** it passes, with a specific new date and the reason. A breach discovered by the customer is a second complaint; a breach we announced is usually accepted. Every breach records what caused the delay, and where the cause was ours the customer receives goodwill under retention §3 without asking. Severity is never lowered to bring a target back into reach (severity §4), and a target is never met by sending an empty reply — a response that says nothing to keep the clock green is a worse breach than a late answer, because it hides the delay from the people who could fix it.

## 5. Waiting on the Customer

The clock pauses only while we are genuinely waiting on something the customer must supply and we have asked for it clearly and specifically. It does not pause for internal work, for a department referral, for stock, or for a batch investigation. Where a customer goes quiet, chase twice — the second chase spaced at least 3 business days after the first — and if there is still no reply 10 days after the first chase, close with a message saying what we were waiting for, what we would have done, and that a reply reopens the ticket at its original severity. Never close silently (intake §10).

## 6. Escalated Tickets

Where a ticket is referred to another department under escalation §1, the receiving department owes the agent an answer within **2 business days**, and a referral involving a safety or legal trigger is answered same-day. The referral does not change what the customer was promised: the originating agent keeps the customer informed at least every 2 business days while it is open, whether or not there is progress to report — a customer who hears nothing assumes nothing is happening, and is usually right. Where the receiving department will miss its 2 days, the originating agent is told so they can update the customer under §4.

## 7. Nothing Ages Out of Sight

No ticket waits more than **2 business days** in a review or triage state without a human decision, and no ticket sits unworked past its resolution target without either a recorded reason or reassignment. Safety reports are stricter — never more than one business day in review, per product_safety §1. Queue age is reviewed daily against these limits, and the oldest breach is worked before the newest arrival at equal severity (severity §5).

## 8. Outside Business Hours

Critical is covered continuously, including Sundays and holidays, because a safety symptom does not wait for the next business day and an active hazard is not a queue item. Every other severity follows business hours, and the published windows already account for that — a normal complaint arriving Saturday evening is not late on Monday morning. Where volume from a single cause threatens targets across the board, that is a systemic incident and is handled under incident §8, which does not suspend these targets.
