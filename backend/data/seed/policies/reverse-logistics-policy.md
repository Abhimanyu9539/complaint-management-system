# Reverse Pickup & Refund Release Policy

This policy governs how an approved return, exchange or company-error pickup is actually collected, and exactly when the resulting refund is released. Whether the customer is *entitled* to return an item is returns §1–§5; this policy starts once that question is already answered yes.

## 1. Scheduling

A reverse pickup is scheduled within 2 business days of approval, at a slot the customer confirms — not an unannounced attempt. The customer is told what to have ready: the item, its original packaging where required by returns §2, and any accessories.

## 2. Pickup Attempt Limits

A courier makes up to **2 pickup attempts** at the scheduled address before the case is flagged for the self-ship fallback in §3. Where a pickup attempt fails because no one was available, the customer is notified and offered a rescheduled slot before the second attempt is used; a pattern of failed pickups on one account is a fraud §2 signal, not simply logged as customer unavailability.

## 3. Self-Ship Fallback and Reimbursement

Where courier pickup genuinely cannot reach the customer's address, or both attempts under §2 have failed for reasons outside the customer's control, the customer may self-ship the item back. Any agent may authorise reimbursement of the actual self-ship postage cost up to **₹500** without approval; a self-ship cost above that requires lead approval, since it usually signals the item or the address needs a different handling route entirely rather than simply reimbursing whatever it cost.

## 4. Doorstep QC and Rejected Pickups

Where the return reason requires it (a "wrong item" or "not as described" claim, an opened-box condition check under returns §2), the pickup agent performs a quick doorstep quality check against the stated reason before collecting. A pickup may be rejected on the spot only where what is being handed over is clearly not what was approved for return (a different product entirely, visible damage inconsistent with the stated reason) — never on a subjective judgement about condition, which is properly assessed at the warehouse under §5. A rejected pickup is escalated to a lead immediately rather than left as a failed attempt, since it usually means the ticket needs a second look, not just a retry.

## 5. When the Refund Is Released

For orders under **₹5,000**, the refund is released on the courier's pickup scan — the confirmation that the item has been collected — rather than waiting for it to reach the warehouse, because the cost of an occasional bad-faith pickup at this value is lower than the cost of delaying every honest customer's refund by the transit time. For orders at or above ₹5,000, the refund is released once the item is received and passes quality check at the warehouse, which must happen within 3 business days of the item arriving; a QC that takes longer than that without a documented reason releases the refund anyway. Where the warehouse QC finds the returned item does not match what was approved (wrong item, missing accessories, condition inconsistent with the stated reason), the ticket is flagged for review rather than the refund being silently withheld — the customer is told specifically what was found and given a chance to respond before any final decision.

## 6. Exchange Instead of Refund

Where a customer wants an exchange for a different size, variant or colour rather than a refund, the replacement may ship immediately, ahead of the original item's pickup, wherever stock allows — the customer is not made to wait for the full reverse-and-refund cycle just to receive the right item sooner. Where stock does not allow immediate exchange, standard refund timing under §5 applies and the customer re-orders once stock is available, informed proactively rather than left to check back.
