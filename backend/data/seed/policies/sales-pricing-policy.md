# Pricing, MRP & Promotion Policy

This policy governs what a customer is entitled to pay: the price itself, promotions and coupons, and price-match requests. What the order actually contains and its lifecycle after payment is orders §1–§8; what the product page promised beyond price is catalog §1–§5.

## 1. Never Above MRP

No product is ever sold above its declared Maximum Retail Price — this is a requirement under the Legal Metrology Act, not a company policy we could choose to relax. Where a technical error causes a charge above MRP, correct it immediately and refund the difference without requiring the customer to raise a dispute; this is treated as a system fault, the same as a billing error under billing §1. MRP, along with net quantity, manufacturing details and consumer-care contact information, must appear on every listing and in every order confirmation (catalog §4) — an agent who cannot find the MRP on a listing reports it as a catalog defect rather than guessing.

## 2. The Advertised Price Is Binding

If a price was live and visible to the customer at the time they ordered, that is the price they are entitled to, whether or not our systems applied it and whether or not it was the price we intended — provided it does not exceed MRP under §1. A misconfigured promotion, a pricing rule that failed on a bundle, a stale banner, a coupon that did not survive checkout — none of these are the customer's error and none of them transfer to the customer to fix. Honour the advertised price and adjust the order under billing §3. The one exception is a price so far below plausible that it is self-evidently a system fault rather than an offer (a decimal-point error, a currency mix-up); those are declined with an explanation and escalated to sales, never silently cancelled.

## 3. Coupon and Promotion Eligibility

A coupon exclusion only binds a customer if it was published where they could have read it before ordering. If the promotions page said a coupon was active and did not say bundles, multi-item carts or specific SKUs were excluded, the exclusion is not enforceable against a customer who relied on the page — honour the discount and get the page corrected. Conversely, a clearly stated exclusion is enforceable even if a customer objects to it. Expired coupons are not reinstated on request, but a coupon that expired between the customer building their cart and completing checkout is honoured. Never resolve a coupon dispute by telling a customer to cancel and re-order to receive advertised pricing they were eligible for at time of purchase — see orders §2 for why a cancel-and-reorder request also costs the customer their place in the queue. Where a coupon was applied but never actually charged correctly, or needs reissuing after a cancellation, see stored_value §4.

## 4. Price Match and Post-Purchase Price Drops

Where we advertise a lower price on the same SKU within **14 days** of a customer's purchase, they are entitled to the difference as a refund or store credit on request. This covers our own pricing only — competitor price matching is a commercial decision made by sales, not an entitlement, and is declined politely at agent level. Clearance, final-sale and time-boxed flash pricing are excluded from the 14-day window and must be stated as such when the customer asks. One price adjustment per order line.

## 5. Quotes, Bulk and Business Pricing

A written quote for a bulk or business order is valid for **30 days** from issue unless it states otherwise, and is honoured within that period even if list pricing moves. Bulk and business pricing is quoted per order and does not create a standing entitlement to the same rate on the next one. Volume commitments, rebates and channel terms belong to the partner policy (partner §8), not to individual order handling.

## 6. Tax and Invoice Corrections

A customer entitled to a GST-registered business invoice, or a corrected billing name, address or GSTIN, is entitled to a corrected invoice, and the correction is made without a fee and without requiring a return or re-order. Where the correction changes the amount owed, the difference is refunded or charged through billing. Invoices are corrected, never deleted, and the original remains on the record; a credit note is issued for the difference, per payments §8.

## 7. What Sales May Not Promise

Sales sets price and promotion terms, and nothing else. It may not extend a warranty period or vary its coverage (warranty §1, §4.1), guarantee a delivery date beyond the published windows in shipping §1, promise a product feature or fix that is not already shipped (tech_support §4), or agree a return window longer than returns §1 allows. A commitment made at the point of sale that this corpus does not support still binds us to the customer — so it is honoured, then corrected internally as a coaching matter, and the customer is never made to argue about it.
