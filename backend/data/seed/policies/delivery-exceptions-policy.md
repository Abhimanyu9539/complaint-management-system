# Delivery Exceptions & Fulfilment Accuracy Policy

This policy governs what arrived being wrong, incomplete, or not arriving at all despite being in the courier's network — as distinct from shipping §1–§6, which covers timing, loss, and transit damage. If the parcel is simply late or lost, or the contents are damaged rather than wrong, that is shipping. If what arrived does not match what was ordered, or never reaches the customer's hands despite multiple attempts, that is here.

## 1. Wrong Item Shipped

Where the item received does not match the order — wrong model, wrong variant, wrong product entirely — this is company error regardless of what the packing slip says. The correct item ships immediately without waiting for the wrong item's return, a reverse pickup for the wrong item is arranged at no cost, and the customer is never asked to pay a price difference either way. This is treated as a company-caused case under returns §4 for refund-method purposes if the customer prefers a refund instead of the exchange.

## 2. Item Missing From the Parcel

Where a multi-item order arrives with one or more items missing, verify against the packed-item manifest and the parcel's recorded weight where available before responding, but never let that check delay the customer beyond one business day. Once confirmed as a fulfilment shortfall, the missing item is shipped immediately at no cost; it is not treated as a lost-package claim under shipping §2, which concerns a whole shipment failing to arrive, not a partial pack error.

## 3. Partial and Split Delivery

Where an order is fulfilled as a split shipment (common for multi-item or multi-warehouse orders), the customer is told at the time of split that further parcels are coming and given a expected date for each — this is not itself a complaint-worthy event if communicated. It becomes one where a split was not communicated, where one part is significantly delayed beyond the others without explanation, or where the customer was billed for delivery once but the split created additional handling they should not bear the cost of.

## 4. Tampered, Opened or Empty Packaging

Where a parcel arrives with packaging that has clearly been opened, resealed, or is empty or under-weight relative to what was ordered, this is treated with the same urgency as a lost package (shipping §2) — a replacement or refund is offered without waiting for a carrier investigation, and the customer is never asked to prove the parcel was tampered with beyond what they can reasonably show (photos of the packaging as received, before opening further). Where a courier's proof-of-delivery photo itself shows visible tampering or damage at the point of handover, that photo supports the customer's claim rather than being treated as evidence delivery succeeded intact.

## 5. Failed Delivery Attempts and Return to Origin

A courier makes up to **3 delivery attempts** before a parcel is returned to origin (RTO), with the customer notified before each attempt where possible and definitely after the first failed one, so the second and third attempts are not a surprise. Where RTO occurs because the customer was genuinely unreachable, the order is cancelled and, for a prepaid order, refunded in full within the timelines in payments §4 without requiring the customer to explicitly ask; for a COD order, it is simply cancelled, since no payment was collected. Where RTO occurred because of a fault on our or the courier's side — wrong address on the label despite a correct one on the order, no delivery attempt actually made despite being logged as attempted — this is company error and the customer is offered immediate redelivery at no charge under §6, not treated as their missed delivery.

## 6. Redelivery After RTO, and the Waiver

Where a customer wants the order redelivered after an RTO rather than refunded, any agent may waive redelivery charges and rebook it without approval, regardless of whether the original RTO was the customer's fault or ours — the cost of an extra shipment is far smaller than the cost of losing the sale and the goodwill on a customer who still wants the product.

## 7. OTP-Verified Delivery Disputes and Courier Conduct

Where delivery is confirmed via OTP but the customer states they never received or entered one, do not treat the OTP confirmation as conclusive proof against the customer — request the courier's delivery record (recipient name, OTP entry timestamp, any photo) before concluding the claim is unfounded, and where the record is inconclusive or absent, resolve it in the customer's favour as a presumptive non-delivery under shipping §2. A delivery date or time window quoted by a courier's tracking page is the courier's current estimate, not a commitment we have made (comms §4); complaints specifically about a courier partner's conduct — rudeness, refusal to wait, mishandling — are logged against that courier for the fulfilment team's ongoing courier-performance review, separately from whatever remedy the customer's own delivery issue requires.

## 8. Address Correction After Dispatch

Where a customer needs to correct an address after dispatch — as distinct from a pre-dispatch change under orders §2 — attempt a courier re-route first; if the courier's network cannot support a re-route, the parcel is allowed to RTO under §5 and reshipped to the corrected address at no charge once it returns, rather than being treated as a customer-caused failed delivery.
