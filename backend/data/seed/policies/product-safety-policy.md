# Product Safety Policy

This policy governs the handling of reported safety hazards, batch/serial tracking, recall procedures, and the language support staff may use with customers.

## 1. Hazard Reporting & Triage

Any report involving smoke, burning smell, sparking, overheating beyond normal operating temperature, unexpected mechanical failure during use, or physical injury must be triaged as a safety report, not a routine defect, regardless of which department originally received it. Safety reports take priority over standard queue ordering and should never be left in a "needs review" state for more than one business day.

## 2. Batch & Serial Tracking

Every safety report must have the affected unit's serial number logged, including the batch/date-code prefix (e.g. "X200-2025Q1"). This is what allows engineering to determine whether an issue is isolated or part of a broader batch pattern. A single report is treated as isolated; three or more reports sharing a batch prefix within a 30-day window automatically opens an internal batch investigation.

## 3. Recall Criteria & Process

### 3.1 Internal Review vs. Public Recall

A batch under internal safety review is **not** the same as a public recall, and support staff must not use the word "recall" with customers until Legal and Product Safety jointly confirm and announce one. The correct language is "under internal review" or "flagged for quality investigation." Overstating a review as a confirmed recall creates legal and regulatory exposure.

### 3.2 Escalation Trigger

Any batch reaching the three-report threshold in §2, or any single report involving injury, is automatically escalated to Legal per the legal department's injury-claim handling process, in parallel with the standard safety remedy to the customer.

## 4. Immediate Remedies for Safety Concerns

Regardless of the product's standard warranty or return remedy hierarchy, any customer reporting a safety symptom (see §1) is authorized to receive an immediate full refund on request, without requiring the unit's return first if returning it would require continued handling of a hazardous item. Customers should always be given clear instructions to stop using the unit immediately and, where relevant, safe disposal or storage instructions (e.g. battery disposal, not household trash).

## 5. Regulatory Reporting Obligations

Confirmed batch safety issues meeting the §3.2 escalation trigger, or any incident involving injury, must be logged for potential regulatory reporting by the compliance team. Support and product_safety staff are not authorized to make any external regulatory statements themselves — this is strictly a Legal and Compliance function.

## 6. E-Waste, Take-Back & Safe Disposal

Every applicable product carries its BIS registration and marking, and the listing and in-box documentation state this — a customer asking whether a product is compliant is answered from the listing, not left to search for it. As a producer under the E-Waste (Management) Rules, we are obligated to take back end-of-life units and batteries rather than let a customer discard them as household waste. Where a customer asks how to dispose of a unit or a battery — whether prompted by a safety symptom under §4 or simply because the product has reached end of life — offer the take-back route (a reverse pickup at no charge, or drop-off at a listed collection point) before giving any other disposal instruction. Where take-back genuinely cannot reach the customer in a reasonable time, give the safe-interim-storage instruction (do not place in household trash, keep away from heat, tape battery terminals) exactly as already given in practice for safety incidents, and follow up once take-back is available.
