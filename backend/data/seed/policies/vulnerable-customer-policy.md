# Accessible Service & Vulnerable Customer Policy

This policy is company-wide and governs customers for whom our standard process is a barrier rather than a service. Most of our remedies quietly assume a customer who can photograph a unit, hand over an item at the door, complete an OTP-verified checkout, read a policy in English, and navigate an app confidently. Where that assumption fails, the process has to bend.

## 1. Recognise, Do Not Diagnose

Signals worth noticing include a stated disability or impairment, dependence on the product for a health reason, bereavement, financial hardship, evident confusion about a process we consider simple, age-related difficulty with the app or OTP flow, and a language or script barrier. Noticing is the whole job: agents do not assess, categorise, question or record a judgement about anyone's condition. What is recorded is the **adjustment we made**, not the reason we made it (privacy §1, §6). A customer who mentions being unwell has told us how to serve them better, not disclosed a medical history for our files.

## 2. Reasonable Adjustments

Any of these may be offered by any agent, without approval:

- **More time** — extended windows for returns, evidence, or a reply, and no close for non-response while an adjustment is in place (sla §5).
- **A different channel** — a phone call instead of the app or chat, a written summary after a call, large print, or a simplified message.
- **Waiving handling requirements** — no photograph where the customer cannot take one, no OTP-only verification where a customer cannot receive or read one, a pickup arranged without requiring the customer to carry or repackage the item themselves.
- **Shipping the remedy first** and arranging the reverse pickup afterwards, or not requiring it at all.
- **A collection from the door at a time that works**, rearranged without limit for a documented adjustment, in contrast to the standard pickup-attempt limits in reverse §2.

These are adjustments to *how* the remedy is delivered. The remedy itself still comes from the owning policy, and this policy never reduces what a customer is entitled to.

## 3. Dependence and Urgency

Where a customer depends on the product for a health reason — a medical device accessory, a mobility aid, something a treatment or a diet relies on — the ticket rises at least one severity level under severity §6 and the replacement ships on the fastest available service at no charge (shipping §4). A customer telling us they cannot manage without it for a week is telling us something about severity, not asking for a favour, and the delay we would ordinarily consider acceptable is not acceptable for them.

## 4. Someone Acting for the Customer

It is common for an order to be placed, tracked or followed up on by a family member — an adult child managing a parent's account, a spouse handling the household's orders — rather than by the account holder themselves. Verify the relationship **once** — a stated relationship plus the customer's confirmation, or documentation where it exists — record that it is verified, and do not ask again on the next contact. Making a family member re-establish their standing every time is one of the most common and most avoidable failures in this area, and it must not be confused with the identity-verification steps in account §2, which exist for a different reason and a different risk. Where a customer has died, the person handling their affairs is helped without being asked for documents we do not need, and outstanding subscriptions are cancelled with refunds from the date of notification (billing §2) without a save offer of any kind (retention §5).

## 5. No Proof of Vulnerability

We never require evidence of a disability, a diagnosis, a bereavement or a hardship before granting an adjustment, and we never ask a customer to explain a condition in order to qualify. The adjustments in §2 cost us very little; requiring proof costs the customer their dignity and costs us the complaint that follows. This mirrors how stated urgency is handled everywhere else in this corpus (severity §3, shipping §4) — the statement is sufficient.

## 6. Language, Script and Digital Literacy

Reply in the customer's preferred language and script (comms §7), and use an interpreter where one is available rather than asking a family member to translate a decision. **Never rely on the customer to translate a safety instruction**: where a stop-use message cannot be delivered in a language the customer reads, escalate it rather than sending English and assuming. Where a customer struggles with the app, OTP verification, or online forms generally, offer the phone or a guided walk-through rather than repeating instructions for a channel that is not working for them — comprehension is our responsibility, not theirs, and a customer who did not understand a policy was not told it.

## 7. Distress and Risk to a Person

Where a customer expresses distress beyond the complaint — hopelessness, threats to harm themselves, or threats of violence — the ticket goes to a lead **immediately**, ahead of any queue position, and any assistant-generated draft is reviewed by that lead before anything is sent (ai §4). Acknowledge the person, do not attempt to counsel, advise or assess them, and do not respond to distress with a policy explanation. Where there is an apparent risk to life, the lead follows the emergency-contact procedure; support staff do not make that call alone and are never expected to. An abusive customer may be told the conversation will continue in writing only, which is a boundary rather than a refusal of service — their complaint is still resolved.

## 8. Recording an Adjustment

An adjustment is recorded on the **ticket** so the next person on that ticket does not undo it. It is not recorded on the account unless the customer asks us to remember it for next time — a standing note about a person's health or capacity, made without their asking, is data we were not given permission to keep (privacy §1). Where the customer does ask, the note says what to do — "prefers phone contact", "OTP by call, not SMS" — and not why.
