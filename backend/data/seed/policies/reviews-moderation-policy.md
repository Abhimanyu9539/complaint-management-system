# Review & Q&A Moderation Policy

This policy is company-wide and governs product reviews and question-and-answer content on our listings: what gets removed, what never does regardless of how unwelcome it is, and how the content we collect there feeds back into the rest of the business.

## 1. What Is Removed

Content is removed where it is abusive or threatening toward a person, defamatory of an identifiable individual, contains another person's personal data (a phone number, an address, a full name in a complaint about a specific staff member), is spam or unrelated advertising, or is a duplicate posting of the same review across multiple listings solely to game visibility. Removal is content-based, not sentiment-based: a review may be scathing about the product and stay up in full.

## 2. What Is Never Removed

A genuine negative review about the product, the delivery experience, or our service is never removed for being unflattering, and is never removed on request from anyone internally who simply dislikes it. Removing honest negative feedback to protect a rating is a practice we do not engage in, and no one — including a category manager or a brand lead — may request a review's removal on those grounds alone.

## 3. Incentivised and Solicited Reviews

Reviews obtained in exchange for a discount, a free product, or any other incentive must be labelled as such wherever our platform supports labelling, and a customer offering one is told plainly the label will apply. Directly asking a customer to change a negative review in exchange for a remedy is prohibited — a remedy is owed on its own merits (returns, warranty, etc.) and is never conditioned on the customer editing or removing feedback.

## 4. A Hazard Reported in a Review Is a Safety Report

Where a review or a Q&A answer describes a safety symptom — smoke, burning smell, sparking, overheating, injury — it is routed to product_safety §1 as a safety report with the same urgency as one reaching us through any other channel, in addition to whatever the platform's normal moderation does with the review itself. The review is not simply moderated and left at that; someone has told us about a hazard, and that triggers the same obligations it would through a support ticket.

## 5. Reviews as a Defect Signal

Review text mentioning a defect, a failure, or a specific problem is a secondary quality signal alongside the return-reason data that primarily drives qa §1's investigation trigger. QA periodically scans review text by SKU for defect language and treats a cluster of matching complaints in reviews the same way it would treat a cluster of support tickets — as evidence worth checking, not as noise to be managed down through moderation.

## 6. Responding Publicly Without Conceding

A public response to a negative review follows the same restrictions as any other customer communication: no admission of fault, no promise not authorised elsewhere in this corpus, and no defensive or dismissive tone (comms §1, legal §2). Where the review describes something that should route to Legal under legal §1, the public response acknowledges and invites the reviewer to contact support directly, and says nothing more.

## 7. Moderation Turnaround

A reported review or Q&A item (reported by a customer, a staff member, or flagged automatically) is reviewed within **2 business days**. Content meeting §1's removal criteria is taken down on that review; content that does not is left in place and, where the report was made in good faith about something ambiguous, the reporter may be told why it was kept up.
